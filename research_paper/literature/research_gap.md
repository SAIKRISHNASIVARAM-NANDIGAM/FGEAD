# Research Gap Analysis
## FGEAD: Feature-level Graph-based Explainable Anomaly Detection

---

## Existing Research

The literature on multivariate time-series anomaly detection has advanced substantially across several well-established directions:

### Detection Architectures
Methods such as OmniAnomaly [12], USAD [13], MSCRED [14], and Anomaly Transformer [15] have demonstrated effective anomaly detection capabilities on multivariate time series. These approaches use stochastic recurrent models, adversarial autoencoders, multi-scale signature matrices, or attention association discrepancies to flag anomalous observations.

### Graph-Based Telemetry Modeling
GDN [8] and MTAD-GAT [6] have shown that modeling inter-metric dependencies as graphs improves detection in multivariate settings. GANF [9] extends graph modeling by integrating Bayesian causal graph discovery with normalizing flows.

### Post-Hoc Explainability Approaches
Standard XAI tools (SHAP [3], LIME [2]) have been applied to time-series anomaly detection models [11]. GNNExplainer [16] and PGExplainer [17] provide explanation mechanisms specifically for graph neural networks. InterFusion [7] offers MCMC-based metric localization.

### Benchmarking
Exathlon [10] provides a benchmark framework for evaluating time-series explainability, establishing evaluation paradigms for root-cause localization quality.

## Limitations and Operational Challenges

Critical analysis of the literature highlights several key operational challenges when deploying existing models in enterprise server environments:

### 1. Challenges of Generic XAI Tools on Telemetry Data
Applying generic feature-attribution tools (SHAP [3], LIME [2]) to multivariate time-series telemetry presents notable challenges:
- Telemetry metrics are often strongly interdependent, violating independence assumptions.
- Temporal context within sliding windows must be preserved; perturbation schemes can create unrealistic metric combinations.
- Repeated explanation over high-frequency sliding windows is computationally demanding.
- Raw feature importance vectors do not automatically provide relationship-level interpretation or operational alert formatting.

### 2. Post-Hoc Auditing Beyond Attention Weights
While models such as GDN [8] and MTAD-GAT [6] learn feature dependency representations, Jain and Wallace [18] demonstrated that raw attention weights alone do not reliably equal explanation. Additional auditing is required to determine how empirical metric correlations behave during anomalous windows relative to learned dependency representations.

### 3. Multi-Dimensional Operational Alerting
When an anomaly is flagged, operations engineers require answers across multiple diagnostic dimensions:
- *Which* specific metrics deviated (with scale-aware Z-score ranking)?
- *What* was the direction of deviation ($\uparrow$ spike vs. $\downarrow$ drop)?
- *Which* metric relationship patterns altered (decoupling vs. sign reversal)?
- *When* did the anomaly start and end?
- *How* confident is the alert signal?

## Research Gap Formulation

Based on the literature reviewed in this study, existing approaches do not appear to provide a unified framework that simultaneously combines:
1. Dynamic graph structure learning with Top-$K$ sparsification for spatio-temporal forecasting;
2. Scale-aware metric deviation ranking with directional labeling ($\uparrow/\downarrow$);
3. Systematic auditing of learned dependency representations against observed window correlations (Graph Relationship Auditing);
4. Multi-factor composite alert confidence calibration; and
5. Integrated REST API and visual dashboard prototyping for operational decision support.

FGEAD addresses this research gap by combining these capabilities into a single, operationally oriented framework.

---

## Academic Positioning & Qualified Claims

- **Framework Combination:** FGEAD is positioned as an integrated framework combining spatio-temporal graph modeling with structured post-hoc auditing.
- **Diagnostic Hypotheses:** Explanations generated by FGEAD represent candidate diagnostic hypotheses rather than confirmed physical root causes.
- **Non-Causal Relationship Auditing:** Graph Relationship Auditing measures discrepancies between learned dependency representations and empirical Pearson correlations; it does not perform causal discovery.
- **Evaluation Context:** FGEAD's performance is evaluated on SMD Machine 1-1 and synthetic telemetry data; generalization across all machines and datasets remains an open research direction.
